# Creative Brief — Artifact 011: The Drop

**Public title:** _Do heavier objects fall faster?_
**Domain:** Crucible · **Artifact type:** Mirror · **Duration:** 30s (9:16)

## 1. The one idea

Near Earth's surface, gravity gives objects the same gravitational acceleration when air resistance can be ignored. A feather falls slowly in normal air mainly because drag matters enormously for its shape and mass; remove the air and the feather and rock fall together.

## 2. The unforgettable visual

The same drop tower performs two rounds: in air the rock wins while the feather floats behind; then the first pair disappears, the vacuum pair appears, and rock plus feather hit together.

## 3. Composition

- The vertical drop lane fills most of the portrait frame.
- Keep rock and feather side by side with fixed left/right identities.
- No humanoid appears; this phenomenon is entirely object-led.
- Make the second simultaneous landing the strongest visual beat.

## 4. Performance / transformation

Round one should visibly separate the objects within the first second. Round two should stay synchronized from release to impact. The chamber itself is reused, so the difference reads as air versus no air rather than a different experiment.

## 5. Text strategy

Question → air result → drag explanation → vacuum test → answer. Keep the vacuum claim explicit so the episode does not imply feathers always fall like rocks in ordinary air.

## 6. Reusable production assets

Establishes `environment.dropLab`, `prefab.featherRock`, `audio.sfx.dropRelease`, and `audio.sfx.dropImpact`. The rig is reusable for drag, terminal velocity, acceleration, and vacuum demonstrations.

## 7. Success criteria

- In-air rock and feather separation is obvious.
- Vacuum pair lands together within the same visual beat.
- No character appears.
- The explanation distinguishes gravity from air drag.
- Both themes preserve all six drop-rig anchors.
