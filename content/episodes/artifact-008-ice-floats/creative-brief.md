# Creative Brief — Artifact 008: The Float

**Public title:** _Why does ice float on water?_
**Domain:** Gravity Well · **Artifact type:** Well · **Duration:** 30s (9:16)

## 1. The one idea

Ice floats because freezing makes water molecules arrange into a more open structure. The same amount of water occupies more volume as ice, so the solid is less dense than the liquid around it. That lower density allows buoyancy from the water to support it.

## 2. The unforgettable visual

The tank is the protagonist. An ice cube drops, splashes, settles at the surface, expands conceptually to reveal the open frozen structure, then rises and gently bobs. No standing character is used because nothing about the explanation requires one.

## 3. Composition

- Keep the transparent tank large enough that the waterline and partial submersion are always readable.
- The ice expansion is conceptual, not literal real-time freezing; it represents the larger volume of the same mass.
- Do not clutter the tank with too many particles. The waterline and ice position are the primary teaching visuals.
- The reusable kitchen environment should remain background context; the tank owns the center of the vertical frame.

## 4. Performance / transformation

There is no humanoid Observer in this episode. The waterline, ice cube, splash, expansion, rise, and buoyancy cue perform the entire explanation. This is the reference object-led composition for future density/fluid episodes.

## 5. Text strategy

Use the phrase "more open structure" rather than implying air gets trapped inside ordinary ice. Keep the causal chain explicit: freezing structure → greater volume for same mass → lower density → floats.

## 6. Reusable production assets

This episode establishes `prefab.floatTank` and further exercises `environment.kitchenLab`, `audio.ambience.kitchen`, and `audio.sfx.waterSplash`. The tank is designed for future density, dissolving, buoyancy, displacement and fluid-pressure episodes.

## 7. Success criteria

- No idle character appears anywhere in the episode.
- The waterline and floating position read instantly in both themes.
- The episode never suggests that ice floats because it contains air bubbles.
- Expansion and density are visually connected before the buoyancy answer.
- The same semantic `tank.water`, `tank.ice` and `tank.surface` anchors work in Voxel and Toybox.
- The complete explanation has enough breathing room at 30 seconds without feeling padded.
