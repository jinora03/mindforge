# Creative Brief — Artifact 005: The Delay

**Public title:** _Why do you see lightning before thunder?_
**Domain:** Signal Ocean · **Artifact type:** Loom · **Duration:** 15s (9:16)

## 1. The one idea

Lightning and thunder begin with the same electrical event, but light travels vastly faster than sound. The flash reaches the viewer almost immediately while the pressure wave is still crossing the air.

## 2. The unforgettable visual

The same distant storm source emits **two signals at once**: a bright light path snaps to the Observer immediately, while a slower expanding pressure/sound signal visibly crosses the gap and arrives several beats later. The delayed reaction is the explanation.

## 3. Composition

- Keep the storm source and the Observer readable in the same vertical composition whenever the speed difference matters.
- Place the storm source high and to one side so both themes have clear negative space for the travelling signals.
- The light path should feel instantaneous and clean; the sound path should occupy visible travel time.
- On the thunder-arrival beat, keep the Observer large enough that the delayed reaction is unmistakable.

## 4. Performance / transformation

The Observer starts curious, reacts immediately to the distant flash, waits while the slower signal crosses the scene, then recoils when thunder finally arrives. The performance should make the delay feel physical rather than merely diagrammatic.

## 5. Text strategy

This first production pass is deliberately **caption-led rather than using the bundled reference narrator**. The existing reference TTS is not considered publish-quality. Production narration can be imported later without changing the episode's visual timing.

- "Why do you see lightning before thunder?"
- "The flash and thunder start together."
- "Light reaches you almost instantly."
- "Sound moves through air at about 343 m/s."
- "Same event. Different speeds."

## 6. Success criteria

- A first-time viewer understands that the flash and thunder share one cause but arrive at different times.
- The fast-light / slow-sound contrast is readable even with all captions hidden.
- The delayed thunder arrival produces a clear cause → wait → reaction rhythm.
- The composition remains readable in both Voxel and Toybox without theme-specific episode logic.
- The episode exports cleanly as a full 9:16 Short and as individual storyboard shots.
