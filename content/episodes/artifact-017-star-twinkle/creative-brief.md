# Creative Brief — Artifact 017: The Twinkle

**Public title:** _Why do stars twinkle but planets usually do not?_
**Domain:** Signal Ocean · **Artifact type:** Signal · **Duration:** 18s (9:16)

## 1. The one idea

Atmospheric turbulence changes the path of incoming light. Stars are effectively point sources, so those fluctuations strongly change their apparent brightness/position; a planet's small disk sends many nearby rays whose fluctuations average together.

## 2. The unforgettable visual

A tiny star pulses and flickers while one narrow ray route zigzags through atmosphere checkpoints; the camera then moves to a visibly larger planet whose many-ray route remains steadier.

## 3. Composition

- Entirely object-led; no humanoid.
- The star and planet must both remain readable in portrait framing by the comparison shot.
- Use actual motion/flicker on the star instead of describing twinkle only in text.
- Observatory scenery establishes place but never blocks the sky comparison.

## 4. Performance / timing

Question 0–2.5s, star flicker 2.5–6.3s, planet comparison 6.3–9.8s, averaging proof 9.8–13.5s, answer 13.5–18s.

## 5. Text strategy

Short captions ride the active light-path comparison. No pause exists just to display text.

## 6. Reusable production assets

Introduces `environment.observatory`, `prefab.starPlanetPair`, `audio.ambience.observatory`, and `audio.sfx.twinkle` for future astronomy, seeing, atmosphere and telescope episodes.

## 7. Success criteria

- Star visibly flickers before ~3s of explanation time.
- Planet remains visually steadier.
- No idle character appears.
- Full episode is 18 seconds.
- Meaningful state/camera/effect changes happen every ~1–2 seconds.
