# Creative Brief — Artifact 016: The Horizon

**Public title:** _Why does the Moon look bigger near the horizon?_
**Domain:** Dream Garden · **Artifact type:** Mirror · **Duration:** 17s (9:16)

## 1. The one idea

The Moon illusion is perceptual: its angular size changes very little, but horizon context provides comparison cues that make it appear larger than the same Moon high in an open sky.

## 2. The unforgettable visual

The horizon Moon and high Moon are brought into one frame and identical measuring guides snap around both, proving they are the same apparent size even though one felt bigger seconds earlier.

## 3. Composition

- No character; the two Moons and landscape context do all the work.
- Start close enough to sell the illusion, then widen for the proof.
- The equal-size guide reveal must be immediate and unmistakable.
- Avoid long night-sky beauty shots that do not advance the explanation.

## 4. Performance / timing

Horizon illusion 0–2.7s, high-sky comparison by 2.7s, measurement proof at ~5.2s, context explanation ~8.7s, answer ~12.6s, hard stop at 17s.

## 5. Text strategy

Each caption is attached to a new comparison state. No separate narration-only pause.

## 6. Reusable production assets

Introduces `environment.nightOverlook`, `prefab.moonPair`, `audio.ambience.night`, and `audio.sfx.compareTick` for perspective, angular-size and night-sky episodes.

## 7. Success criteria

- Both moon discs are exactly the same modeled size.
- The viewer sees the identical guides around both by ~6s.
- No idle humanoid appears.
- Full episode stays at 17 seconds.
