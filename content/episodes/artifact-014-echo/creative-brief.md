# Creative Brief — Artifact 014: The Return

**Public title:** _Why does an echo happen?_
**Domain:** Signal Ocean · **Artifact type:** Signal · **Duration:** 18s (9:16)

## 1. The one idea

An echo is reflected sound. A pulse travels to a distant surface and back; if that round trip takes long enough, the return is heard as a separate sound.

## 2. The unforgettable visual

One bright sound pulse visibly crosses the canyon, flashes against the far cliff, reverses direction, then lands at the listener with a delayed return sound.

## 3. Composition

- Entirely object/environment-led; no humanoid is needed.
- The canyon must remain wide enough to read the complete route.
- The pulse changes direction on-screen instead of relying on text to explain reflection.
- Never hold a static canyon shot for more than about two seconds.

## 4. Performance / timing

Hook by 0.2s, first impulse at ~2.0s, cliff contact by ~4.5s, echo return by ~6.6s, full round-trip explanation by ~9s, answer begins ~13.2s. Hard stop at 18s.

## 5. Text strategy

Question → outbound travel → reflection → delayed round trip → one-line answer. Text overlaps the visible action instead of waiting for it to finish.

## 6. Reusable production assets

Introduces `environment.canyon`, `prefab.soundRig`, `audio.ambience.canyon`, `audio.sfx.echoImpulse`, and `audio.sfx.echoReturn` for future sonar, wave-speed, distance and reflection episodes.

## 7. Success criteria

- The outbound and returning pulse are both visible.
- The viewer can see the full canyon route in portrait framing.
- No idle character appears.
- Meaningful visual change occurs at least every ~1–2 seconds.
- Full export is exactly 18 seconds.
