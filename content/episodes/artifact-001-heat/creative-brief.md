# Creative Brief — Artifact 001: The Heat Engine

**Public title:** _Why Does Your Computer Get Hot?_
**Domain:** Crucible · **Artifact type:** Engine · **Duration:** 15s (9:16)

## 1. The one idea

Computation is physical work. Information moving through switches and resistance consumes energy, and part of that energy leaves the machine as heat.

## 2. The unforgettable visual

The Heat Engine visibly **loads information into a cold core, accelerates, glows hotter under pressure, then opens and releases a square heat shockwave**. The machine should make the invisible energy cost feel mechanical and unavoidable.

## 3. Composition

- Keep the machine as the dominant subject; the information inlet must always read as feeding it.
- Start close enough that the core/mechanism relationship is readable on a phone.
- Use camera movement only to reveal a new machine state: input, pressure, release, resolution.
- Heat should build progressively rather than appearing as one disconnected explosion.

## 4. Performance / transformation

1. Information starts flowing into the machine immediately.
2. The mechanism spins up and the core begins pulsing.
3. Pressure visibly accumulates around the core while the material gets hotter.
4. The shell unfolds to reveal the stressed interior.
5. The heat release produces the biggest visual beat, then the core cools.

## 5. Text strategy

The visuals should establish cause/effect before the narration confirms it:

- "Why does thinking cost heat?"
- "Information enters the engine."
- "Every switch and resistance costs energy."
- "Computation moves energy — some escapes as heat."

## 6. Success criteria

- The viewer can follow input → work → heat without reading every caption.
- `machine.core`, `machine.mechanism`, and `machine.shell` are visually distinct.
- The release is the strongest beat, but it feels earned by the buildup.
- Voxel and Toybox tell the same idea with clearly different visual languages.
