# Creative Brief — Artifact 012: The Glide

**Public title:** _Why does a paper airplane stay in the air?_
**Domain:** Signal Ocean · **Artifact type:** Loom · **Duration:** 28s (9:16)

## 1. The one idea

A paper airplane glides because forward motion lets its wings interact with the air and generate lift while gravity pulls downward. Drag steadily removes speed, so the glide eventually descends; it is not floating or defeating gravity.

## 2. The unforgettable visual

The plane launches alone down the classroom demonstration lane, airflow wraps from its nose across both wings, and the camera rides alongside as the aircraft trades forward speed for a gentle descending glide.

## 3. Composition

- No standing character. The paper plane is the protagonist for the entire episode.
- Start close enough to read the folded geometry, then transition into a long open lane.
- Airflow effects must stay anchored to the wings during motion.
- Keep the final descent visible rather than ending on a static diagram.

## 4. Performance / transformation

Launch quickly, level into a glide, show airflow/pressure around the wings, then let the nose and flight path settle downward as speed is lost. The object itself performs the explanation.

## 5. Text strategy

Question → forward motion → airflow/lift → gravity/drag → answer. Avoid claiming lift simply comes from air moving faster on top; keep the wording physically broad and correct.

## 6. Reusable production assets

Establishes `prefab.paperPlane`, `audio.sfx.paperLaunch`, and `audio.sfx.airflow`. Reuses `environment.classroomLab` for a new purpose instead of creating another generic room.

## 7. Success criteria

- Plane occupies enough of a phone screen to remain readable.
- Airflow stays attached to semantic nose/wing/tail anchors while the plane moves.
- Final descent communicates that a glide is a balance/tradeoff, not endless suspension.
- No idle humanoid appears.
