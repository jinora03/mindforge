# Creative Brief — Artifact 004: The Cell

**Public title:** _Why does cold kill your battery?_
**Domain:** Crucible · **Artifact type:** Core · **Duration:** 15s (9:16)

## 1. The one idea

A cold battery usually still contains charge; the problem is delivery. Lower temperature slows the chemical/ionic movement inside the cell, so it cannot supply current as quickly.

## 2. The unforgettable visual

A warm cell begins with **fast information-like ion traffic**, then a cold clamp closes around the core and the same traffic visibly stalls. The charge does not disappear—the pathway becomes sluggish.

## 3. Composition

- Treat the cell as the central machine and the two side nodes as the endpoints of the flow.
- Make the warm state and cold state visually comparable from similar framing.
- The cold clamp should feel constricting, not explosive.
- The final pullback should reveal that the system remains intact even though output has sagged.

## 4. Transformation

1. Warm cell powers up.
2. Ions/information move freely.
3. Cold pressure closes in and the core dims.
4. Flow stalls and output visibly sags.
5. The answer clarifies that the charge remains but cannot move fast enough.

## 5. Text strategy

- "Why does cold kill your battery?"
- "Charge moves as ions drifting through a liquid."
- "Cold thickens that liquid and slows every ion down."
- "The charge is still there. It just can't get out fast enough."

## 6. Success criteria

- Warm vs cold flow is a clear before/after comparison.
- Cold reads as slowed transport, not the battery magically losing all stored energy.
- The main cell remains readable throughout the vertical composition.
- Voxel and Toybox preserve the same causal sequence.
