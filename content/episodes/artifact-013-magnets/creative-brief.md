# Creative Brief — Artifact 013: The Selector

**Public title:** _Why do magnets attract some metals but not all?_
**Domain:** Crucible · **Artifact type:** Mirror · **Duration:** 34s (9:16)

## 1. The one idea

A magnet strongly attracts ferromagnetic materials such as iron and ordinary steel because their microscopic magnetic domains can align with the external magnetic field. Being a metal by itself is not enough: materials such as ordinary aluminium do not snap toward a simple magnet the way steel does.

## 2. The unforgettable visual

One horseshoe magnet performs three tests across the same material bench: aluminium does not move, wood does not move, then the steel sample suddenly snaps toward the magnet with a sharp magnetic click.

## 3. Composition

- Keep all three targets visible early so the viewer can predict what will happen.
- Move the magnet, not a human hand, between tests; this is an object-led material experiment.
- The steel snap should be the only large movement until the answer beat.
- Reuse Material Lab from Artifact 006 to make the environment feel like a real production library.

## 4. Performance / transformation

Magnet approaches aluminium, pauses, leaves it untouched; repeats over wood; then approaches steel and pulls it decisively. Finish by visualizing the field/interaction between the magnet tip and steel.

## 5. Text strategy

Question → not every metal → aluminium test → steel test → magnetic-domain answer. Avoid saying non-ferromagnetic metals have literally zero magnetic response; the claim is about strong everyday attraction.

## 6. Reusable production assets

Establishes `prefab.magnetKit` and `audio.sfx.magnetSnap`; reuses `environment.materialLab`, `audio.ambience.lab`, and `audio.sfx.materialTap`.

## 7. Success criteria

- The steel attraction is physically unmistakable.
- Aluminium and wood stay visibly still during their tests.
- No idle character appears.
- The answer identifies ferromagnetic material/domain alignment rather than simply saying “metal attracts magnet.”
