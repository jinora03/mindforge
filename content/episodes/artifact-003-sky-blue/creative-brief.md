# Creative Brief — Artifact 003: The Prism

**Public title:** _Why is the sky blue?_
**Domain:** Signal Ocean · **Artifact type:** Loom · **Duration:** 15s (9:16)

## 1. The one idea

White sunlight carries many wavelengths. Air scatters shorter visible wavelengths more strongly, so blue light reaches your eyes from many directions across the sky.

## 2. The unforgettable visual

A clean incoming light path enters the air, then **breaks into a broad sideways blue scatter that surrounds the Observer**. The transformation from one incoming direction to many outgoing directions is the explanation.

## 3. Composition

- Keep the Observer present as a scale/reference point rather than making the scene a detached diagram.
- The sunlight source must read as one incoming stream before scattering begins.
- Leave negative space around the scatter so its directional change is obvious.
- The final reveal should make blue feel distributed across the environment, not attached to one object.

## 4. Performance

The Observer begins curious, reaches/points toward the light path, then relaxes into the final reveal. Character motion supports the explanation but should never compete with the scattering event.

## 5. Text strategy

- "Why is the sky blue?"
- "Sunlight arrives carrying every colour at once."
- "Air molecules deflect short wavelengths hardest."
- "So blue reaches your eyes from every direction."

## 6. Success criteria

- A viewer sees one incoming signal become many blue directions.
- The explanation remains readable without realistic atmospheric simulation.
- The final frame feels expansive and satisfying on a vertical phone screen.
- Both themes preserve the same signal/scatter story.
