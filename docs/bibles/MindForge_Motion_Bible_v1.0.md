# MindForge Motion Bible

*The pacing, camera, transformation, contour, transition, and Three.js choreography system.*

| **VERSION 1.0** | **MOTION SYSTEM** | **LIVING DOCUMENT** |
|-----------------|-------------------|---------------------|

## 1. Motion philosophy

MindForge motion is not decoration. It is the explanation made visible.

> **Primary rule**  
> Every visible movement must reveal, compare, route, compress, divide, assemble, transform, or resolve something.

- Camera motion creates curiosity; object motion creates meaning.
- A scene may be calm, but it cannot be idle.
- The viewer should understand the direction of cause and effect even with sound muted.
- Motion should feel precise and alive, not mechanically repetitive or randomly procedural.

## 2. Short-form pacing

Retention requires frequent meaningful change, not constant chaos.

| **Time scale**  | **Required event**                                                                  |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 0.0–0.7 s       | A readable contradiction, question, impossible image, or immediate transformation.  |
| Every 0.8–1.8 s | A new beat: activation, route change, split, reveal, camera answer, or text update. |
| Every 3–5 s     | A major change in scale, mechanism, state, or viewpoint.                            |
| Final 1–2 s     | Resolution image, brand echo, or new question without weakening the answer.         |

> **Change is not noise**  
> Rotating the same object, adding more particles, or increasing bloom does not count as a new beat unless it changes what the viewer understands.

## 3. Motion verbs

Use verbs to direct animation. “Make it cinematic” is not a usable instruction.

| **Verb** | **Meaning**                                                 | **Useful for**                               |
|----------|-------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Assemble | Separate pieces form an understandable system.              | Computation, memory, networks, organisms.    |
| Route    | Signals choose or follow paths.                             | Internet, nerves, causality, logistics.      |
| Compress | Spacing decreases and intensity rises.                      | Heat, pressure, probability, attention.      |
| Fracture | One system splits into parts or alternatives.               | Failure, choices, bias, uncertainty.         |
| Fold     | A large structure reveals hidden internal relationships.    | Dimensions, memory, maps, time.              |
| Bloom    | A simple seed becomes a complex connected form.             | Learning, growth, consciousness, ecosystems. |
| Invert   | Inside becomes outside or cause and effect appear reversed. | Paradox, perception, feedback loops.         |
| Cascade  | One event triggers many downstream events.                  | Chain reactions, virality, computation.      |
| Resonate | Systems synchronize and amplify each other.                 | Sound, social influence, neural activity.    |
| Settle   | A chaotic field reaches a readable stable state.            | Resolution, equilibrium, understanding.      |

## 4. Camera grammar

The camera behaves like curiosity: it asks, follows, inspects, and reveals.

| **Shot**        | **Purpose**                                              | **Rule**                                                            |
|-----------------|----------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Breach          | Immediate entry through a contour wall, gate, or object. | Use for hooks; reveal the hero within the first second.             |
| Approach        | Move toward a contradiction or active mechanism.         | The target must grow in meaning, not only size.                     |
| Orbit reveal    | Expose relationships hidden from the first angle.        | Orbit no more than needed; stop when the new relationship is clear. |
| Dive / tunnel   | Follow information into an artifact or through scale.    | Use a strong central path and avoid disorienting spins.             |
| Cross-section   | Open or slice geometry to show internal cause.           | Prefer animated separation over a static cutaway.                   |
| Chase           | Follow a signal, particle, or decision through a system. | The viewer must know what is being followed and why.                |
| Scale flip      | Transform a small unit into a vast world or vice versa.  | Match a shape or contour so the transition feels inevitable.        |
| Pullback answer | Reveal the whole system after showing details.           | Use near the conclusion to make the relationship understandable.    |

### Camera prohibitions

- No uninterrupted 15–30 second Catmull-Rom drift through the same composition.
- No orbiting a symmetrical object simply because the scene looks attractive.
- No handheld shake unless instability is part of the concept.
- No rapid rotation that harms readability on a phone.
- No camera move without a visible target, question, or reveal.

## 5. Contour animation grammar

Contour fields are motion graphics, information, and brand signature at the same time.

| **Animation**    | **State communicated**                 | **Implementation idea**                                         |
|------------------|----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Pulse outward    | Emission, discovery, propagation       | Expand line radius while reducing opacity and line weight.      |
| Compress inward  | Heat, pressure, attention, probability | Reduce spacing around the hero and increase local brightness.   |
| Advection / flow | Information follows a direction        | Offset contour phase along a vector field or spline.            |
| Bend around mass | Gravity, influence, bias               | Warp line vertices with a radial or directional displacement.   |
| Interfere        | Two systems overlap                    | Blend two fields and reveal constructive/destructive regions.   |
| Rupture          | Breakthrough, failure, transition      | Create a discontinuity followed by geometry emergence.          |
| Settle           | Resolution or equilibrium              | Reduce noise, increase spacing consistency, and lower velocity. |

## 6. Transition language

Transitions should carry meaning between shots instead of hiding an edit.

| **Transition**      | **Use**                                                                         |
|---------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Contour tunnel      | Enter a hidden layer, domain, memory, or internal mechanism.                    |
| Geometry match cut  | Match a circle, prism, lattice, or contour shape across scale or location.      |
| Field rupture       | Move from stable understanding into surprise, failure, or a new state.          |
| Signal wipe         | Let a moving thread, wave, or particle reveal the next scene.                   |
| Scale portal        | Turn a tiny component into the next environment without a hard cut.             |
| White insight flash | Mark a key answer only; avoid using it for ordinary edits.                      |
| Settle dissolve     | End a sequence by allowing fields and geometry to resolve into a simpler image. |

## 7. Fifteen-second episode template

Use this for style tests, fast mysteries, and high-retention questions.

| **Time**  | **Beat**       | **Visual job**                                              |
|-----------|----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| 0.0–0.7   | Hook           | Show an impossible contradiction and the question.          |
| 0.7–2.2   | Arrival        | Breach into the artifact; establish hero and contour field. |
| 2.2–5.0   | Activation     | Inputs assemble, route, or collide.                         |
| 5.0–8.5   | Hidden force   | Contours compress, bend, split, or reveal the problem.      |
| 8.5–12.0  | Transformation | Major scale flip or cross-section makes the cause visible.  |
| 12.0–14.2 | Answer         | One clear sentence and a stable final relationship.         |
| 14.2–15.0 | Echo           | Artifact label, MindForge mark, or distant response.        |

## 8. Thirty-second episode template

Use this when the concept needs comparison, a misconception, or a second explanatory layer.

| **Time** | **Beat**                | **Visual job**                                               |
|----------|-------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| 0–1      | Hook                    | Question plus immediate abnormal behavior.                   |
| 1–4      | Set up                  | Establish artifact, input, and expected outcome.             |
| 4–8      | First mechanism         | Show the normal process working.                             |
| 8–12     | Problem / misconception | Reveal what the viewer cannot see in reality.                |
| 12–17    | Internal journey        | Dive, chase, or cross-section through the mechanism.         |
| 17–22    | Cause and effect        | Use contours and state color to show why the outcome occurs. |
| 22–26    | Comparison              | Show “more / less,” “with / without,” or “before / after.”   |
| 26–29    | Resolution              | Pull back to a readable whole-system answer.                 |
| 29–30    | Echo                    | Brand or next curiosity beat.                                |

## 9. Example choreography: computer heat

This replaces the “beautiful screensaver” approach with a sequence where every beat changes understanding.

| **Time**  | **Shot and action**                                                                                             | **Meaning**                                              |
|-----------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| 0.0–0.7   | A cold violet contour lattice snaps into view. One white tile enters; the field instantly compresses.           | Computation has a physical cost.                         |
| 0.7–3.0   | Camera breaches the lattice. Tiles collide with gates; each switch releases a pink ripple.                      | Transistors repeatedly change state.                     |
| 3.0–6.0   | Signals route faster. More gates activate; contour spacing tightens and the artifact warms.                     | More activity creates more energy dissipation.           |
| 6.0–9.0   | Cross-section: resistance nodes divert small parts of each signal into rising rose waves.                       | Electrical resistance and switching losses become heat.  |
| 9.0–12.0  | Scale flip: the tiny waves become a vast hot field surrounding the entire artifact.                             | Small losses accumulate across billions of operations.   |
| 12.0–14.2 | Cooling contours pull heat outward; the system stabilizes. Text: “Computation moves energy. Some becomes heat.” | Cooling removes heat; it does not eliminate energy loss. |
| 14.2–15.0 | ARTIFACT 001 / THE HEAT ENGINE. A distant artifact pulses once.                                                 | Brand echo and connected universe.                       |

## 10. Sound-motion relationship

Sound should confirm motion and scale, not compete with narration or captions.

| **Motion event**         | **Sound family**                                     |
|--------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Contour pulse            | Soft synth swell or glassy wave.                     |
| Geometry lock / assemble | Precise click, snap, or low mechanical chime.        |
| Scale flip               | Deep tonal drop, rising whoosh, or momentary vacuum. |
| Field rupture            | Short distortion, crack, or harmonic break.          |
| Insight state            | Clean high tone layered over a warm low pulse.       |
| Settling                 | Receding texture, softened rhythm, lower density.    |

> **Sync rule**  
> Major visual events should land on sound beats, but the animation must remain understandable with audio muted.

## 11. Three.js production rules

The prototype should help test style and pacing without becoming a one-off wall of code.

| **Area**        | **Rule**                                                                                                    |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Aspect ratio    | Design natively for 9:16. Use 1080×1920 for final capture or 720×1280 for fast tests.                       |
| Timeline        | Use explicit shot and event objects with start, duration, easing, and state; avoid one global camera curve. |
| Camera          | Create reusable shot functions: breach, approach, orbit reveal, dive, cross-section, scale flip, pullback.  |
| Geometry        | Use instancing for repeated tiles, particles, contours, and lattice elements.                               |
| Materials       | Centralize brand materials and colors; do not recreate random emissive materials per scene.                 |
| Post-processing | Use bloom, vignette, grain, and chromatic aberration sparingly; effects cannot replace choreography.        |
| Contours        | Build one reusable contour-field module that supports flow, compression, bend, rupture, and settling.       |
| Performance     | Prefer a stable frame rate and readable silhouettes over dense geometry or excessive particles.             |
| Capture         | Prototype through screen recording or browser capture; later automate frame rendering and video assembly.   |

### Recommended scene data model

**Conceptual structure (not a mandatory final schema):**

> episode  
> metadata: topic, artifact, duration, palette  
> shots\[\]: start, duration, cameraPreset, target  
> events\[\]: start, duration, verb, object, parameters, easing  
> fields\[\]: source, behavior, intensity, colorState  
> text\[\]: start, end, content, placement  
> audioCues\[\]: time, family, intensity

## 12. Motion review checklist

Review at full size, phone size, and muted playback.

| ☐ Hook changes the frame within 0.7 seconds        | ☐ New meaningful beat every 0.8–1.8 seconds          |
|----------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| ☐ Major change every 3–5 seconds                   | ☐ Camera reveals rather than drifts                  |
| ☐ Hero remains readable on a phone                 | ☐ Contours communicate a specific state              |
| ☐ Color change has a reason                        | ☐ At least one major transformation                  |
| ☐ No effect substitutes for explanation            | ☐ Animation works without sound                      |
| ☐ Sound lands on major events                      | ☐ Final frame resolves the core relationship         |
| ☐ No repeated motion that feels like a screensaver | ☐ Performance remains stable during the busiest beat |

## Version 1.0 motion decisions

- Motion is directed by verbs and conceptual purpose.
- Shots are discrete, choreographed reveals rather than one continuous camera path.
- Contour fields are the primary explanatory motion system.
- Fifteen-second prototypes are the default test format; expand only when the idea requires it.
- Three.js architecture should separate creative timelines from rendering boilerplate.
